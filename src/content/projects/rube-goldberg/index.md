**Goal:** Build one segment of a class-wide Rube Goldberg machine on a 48 x 48 in plywood board. It had to be triggered by a box fan 4 ft away (and not by ordinary room airflow) and end by revealing the letters "L", "M" and "U" one at a time.

**Design:** Each team member sketched a concept, and we scored them on functionality, tempo, surprise and comedy. My concept scored highest and became the base for the team design. The fan pushes a cart with a cardboard sail into a cup. The cup pulls a pulley that releases a steel ball. The ball zig-zags down a set of ramps and knocks a cardboard tab off a string, which unrolls a weighted LMU banner.

**Problems we solved:**

- The ball kept flying off the ramps, so we added side rails and backstops
- The banner wouldn't unroll the same way every time. After trying screws, pipe cleaners and a cup rim as guides, we got a reliable release by using a single screw as the only contact point for the string

**Result:** The machine triggered from the fan and revealed LMU every time at the class demo. We went from concept to a working build in about four weeks, tracked in a team work journal.
